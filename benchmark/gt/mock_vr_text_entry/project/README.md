# VRType: Ray-casting vs. Hand-tracking Swipe for VR Text Entry

An HCI user study from the HCI Lab (HCIL) at Hanbit University, comparing two text-entry methods in virtual reality on speed, errors, arm fatigue, and user experience.

**Lead researcher:** 박서연 (Park Seoyeon), MSc student
**Advisor:** 김태호 (Kim Taeho), professor
**Prototype development:** 이도윤 (Lee Doyun)
**Data analysis:** 정하은 (Jung Haeun)
**Pilot facilitation and RA support:** 최민준 (Choi Minjun)

## Research question

Does hand-tracking swipe typing in VR enter text faster than ray-casting on a virtual keyboard, and what does that speed cost in accuracy and physical comfort?

Prior work (Han & Oyelaran, 2023, n = 20) found ray-casting ahead of mid-air tap typing (17.2 vs. 12.9 WPM) and reported arm fatigue increasing after about 10 minutes. That study recommended testing gesture and swipe input, which this project does.

## Hypotheses

- **H1:** Swipe is faster than ray (lower seconds per task).
- **H2:** Swipe produces more errors than ray (a speed-accuracy trade-off).

Both hypotheses were supported in the final analysis.

## Conditions

- **ray**: Ray-casting keyboard. A virtual QWERTY panel 0.7 m away with 2.6 cm keys. A ray from the dominant-hand controller selects keys with the trigger.
- **swipe**: Hand-tracking swipe keyboard. Bare-hand tracking at 60 Hz. A pinch starts a stroke, the fingertip glides over letters, and release ends the stroke. The stroke is matched against a 20,000-word lexicon and the top three candidates are shown.

The headset is the Aurora V2, which has built-in hand tracking and controllers.

## Design

- Within-subjects, 2 conditions x 4 tasks, N = 16 (P01-P16).
- Latin-square counterbalancing: 8 participants started with ray and 8 with swipe.
- Tasks always run in the order T1-T4 within a condition.
- A 3-minute practice block precedes each condition.
- Tasks:
  - **T1:** copy a short common phrase (5 words)
  - **T2:** copy a two-sentence message (about 60 characters)
  - **T3:** enter an 8-character password-like string with one digit and one symbol
  - **T4:** enter a URL, such as `hanbit.ac.kr/lab/news`

Each session lasts at most 60 minutes. Participants can stop at any time.

## Measures

- Seconds per task (first stroke or press to Enter)
- Errors per task (backspace-corrected errors plus uncorrected wrong characters or words)
- Derived speed in words per minute (WPM), computed from T1 and T2
- 7-point Likert survey after each condition (Q1-Q8: ease of use, perceived speed, perceived accuracy, arm fatigue, mental demand, frustration, enjoyment, willingness to use again)
- A 10-minute semi-structured interview at the end of each session

## Participants

16 adults aged 20-34 (mean 25.6), 7 women and 9 men, all right-handed. 11 had used VR before. Each received 15,000 KRW. No one was excluded. P09 had a brief tracking loss during swipe T2, and that data was kept.

## Pilot and changes

The pilot ran from 2026-08-25 to 2026-08-28 with 4 participants on app v0.3.2.

- **Task change:** T3 originally used 12-character random strings. In the pilot, 3 of 4 participants gave up or took over 2 minutes, so T3 was shortened to the 8-character string described above.
- **Bug fix:** In swipe mode on v0.3.2, pressing Backspace did not clear the previous stroke buffer, so the last word was duplicated on the next stroke. v0.3.3 calls `clear_stroke_buffer()` on Backspace. The main study used v0.3.3.

The main study ran from 2026-09-14 to 2026-10-02.

## Results summary

**Time:** Swipe was faster. Mean time per task was 41.8 s (SD 9.6) for ray and 33.2 s (SD 8.1) for swipe, a 20.6% reduction. Two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on per-participant means: W = 7, p = .002, r = 0.79.

**Errors:** Swipe produced more errors. Mean errors per task were 1.9 (SD 1.1) for ray and 3.4 (SD 1.6) for swipe, 79% more. Wilcoxon: W = 10, p = .004, r = 0.74.

**Speed (WPM, from T1 and T2):** ray 15.1, swipe 19.0.

**Survey:** Swipe was rated higher for perceived speed (Q2, p = .003) and enjoyment (Q7, p = .004), and it produced more arm fatigue (Q4, p = .011). Ray was rated higher for perceived accuracy (Q3, p = .021). The survey tests are uncorrected. The findings write-up embeds the Likert means chart, which is generated by the survey analysis script.

## Running the analysis

Run from the project root:

```
python analysis/analyze_logs.py --input <path to the task log CSV>
```

This prints per-condition means and the Wilcoxon results. The survey script saves the Likert figure to `results/figures/`.
